Passed,Not Paid.

A law is a promise. An appropriation is whether anyone meant it.

Every case below is a federal law that passed — signed, on the books — where the money to carry it out was never fully appropriated. We sort them by how little arrived: crumbs (zero, or a token under 5% of the promise) and pennies on the dollar (funded, but far below what the law pledged). Not a conspiracy — a gap between passed and paid, and a name for who carries it.

How this is built

Prove-it. Every case is drawn from the public record — statutes, appropriations acts, CRS/GAO analyses, and reporting that cites them. No FOIA, no invented figures: every amount traces to the statute or an authoritative source, and each card carries its citations. Where a figure comes from an advocate, a legislator, or a third-party aggregator, the card says so.

Two tiers, one bar. Crumbs is the strict core — authorized or mandated, but $0 or ≤~5% appropriated. Pennies on the dollar is funded, but far below the statutory promise (IDEA's ~13% against a 40% commitment; LWCF at roughly half). Keeping them separate keeps the strict core unassailable.

Not the mirror image. This tracks laws authorized but not funded (promise withheld). It is not CBO's "expired authorizations" list — programs funded without a current authorization ("zombie" spending). Those are opposites; that data is a candidate for a separate future lane, not this one.

Non-partisan by construction — the cases span laws signed by both parties. Phase 1 is federal. State and local gaps (where the matching is harder) come in a later phase.
